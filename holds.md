# Answer key holds

## data/anatomy/gross-curated.json

- **dq-g-gv1** (keyed A T, B T, C T, D F, E T). The key treats the portal route as a third independent circulation and makes an internal elastic lamina a universal component of the intima. The hepatic portal route is part of systemic circulation, and a distinct lamina is characteristic of arteries rather than all vessels. Check the intended course convention.
- **dq-g-gv5** (keyed B (kidney)). The kidney is the intended answer, with fenestrated glomerular endothelium and a continuous basal lamina, but adrenal medullary sinusoids are also described as fenestrated with a continuous basal lamina. Specify which histological definition of discontinuous is intended, or replace the adrenal option.
- **dq-g-gv6** (keyed A T, B T, C T, D T, E F). The vein-wall statement keyed true excludes an elastic lamina altogether, but histological descriptions recognise a thin or inconspicuous internal elastic lamina in some veins. Qualify the claim before explaining it as a rule.
- **dq-g-gvs9** (keyed A (Right, passing under the aortic arch)). The right pulmonary artery is longer and larger, but the keyed answer adds that it passes under the aortic arch. The defining right-sided route is behind the ascending aorta and superior vena cava, while the left pulmonary artery emerges within the concavity of the arch. Clarify the intended relation and revise the option if needed.
- **dq-g-gvg11** (keyed C (left bronchus and descending aorta)). The left pulmonary artery is anterior to the descending aorta, but its relation to the left main bronchus changes along its course. At the root it arches superior to the left main bronchus before descending posterolaterally. Specify the segment if retaining the keyed anterior relation.
- **dq-g-gvh1** (keyed E (portal system)). The key names the hepatic portal pathway as a third circulation alongside systemic and pulmonary. Standard anatomy treats it as a specialised route within the systemic circulation. Confirm the course-specific classification and state it in the stem if intended.
- **dq-g-gvh3** (keyed B (an elastic lamina in collagenous tissue)). The elastic lamina is prominent in many arterial intimas, but is inconspicuous or absent in many veins, and capillaries have no equivalent multilayered intima. Specify an artery or the vessel type intended by the keyed description.
- **dq-g-gvh4** (keyed C (metarteriole)). The key equates an arteriovenous shunt with a metarteriole. In the capillary-bed model a metarteriole can feed the throughfare channel, while direct arteriovenous anastomoses are a distinct arrangement. Check the lecture terminology and clarify which structure is intended.
- **dq-g-gvh8** (keyed B (Ulnar artery)). The keyed ulnar artery is clinically palpable at the wrist, and the stem does not identify a specific list. If it tests the lecture's chosen list, cite that list or replace the distractor with a genuinely nonpalpable artery.
- **dq-g-gvh19** (keyed C (median sacral artery)). The median sacral artery arises from the posterior aspect of the aorta near the bifurcation. The coeliac, superior mesenteric and inferior mesenteric arteries are the three unpaired anterior visceral branches, so there is no correct fourth ventral branch among the options.
- **dq-g-go3** (keyed A T, B T, C T, D T, E F). The stem includes blood and lymphatic vessels, but the key applies the mucosa–muscularis–adventitia model and immediate luminal epithelium across hollow organs. Vascular channels have endothelium and their own tunics, while many digestive segments have a distinct submucosa. Specify the system before keying the general wall plan.
- **dq-g-gos2** (keyed B (epithelium and lamina propria)). Epithelium and lamina propria describe a mucosa, but the stem applies to all tubular organs. The vascular tunica intima, for example, has endothelium and subendothelial tissue rather than a lamina propria. Specify a mucosa-lined tract.
- **dq-g-gog3** (keyed C (connective tissue between muscle layers)). The stem gives no organ. In the gut, major vessels and the submucosal plexus are chiefly in submucosa, while the myenteric plexus lies between muscularis externa layers. The keyed location describes only part of the distribution and cannot be applied generally.
- **dq-g-gog9** (keyed D (superior mediastinum)). The course described is that of the left superior intercostal vein, which crosses the aortic arch. The highest or supreme intercostal vein principally drains the first intercostal space and usually reaches the brachiocephalic vein separately. Clarify the intended vessel.
- **dq-g-gog15** (keyed E (azygos system)). Pericardial veins drain via both azygos tributaries and pericardiacophrenic veins that reach the brachiocephalic veins. The sources confirm the azygos pathway but do not establish that it carries most pericardial drainage. Specify the territory or ask for one route.
- **dq-g-goh7** (keyed C (Respiratory system)). Wall coats vary among different airway segments, but other systems in the options also have substantial segmental variation. The stem asks for a teaching example without supplying the classification or specifying features such as cartilage. Confirm the intended lecture framing.
- **dq-g-goh22** (keyed C (venae cavae and pulmonary veins)). The oblique sinus is the blind recess between pulmonary venous reflections and the inferior vena cava behind the left atrium. The keyed option says both venae cavae, but the superior vena cava relates to the transverse sinus rather than bounding the oblique recess. Change the option to inferior vena cava and pulmonary veins.
- **dq-g-goh41** (keyed B (right 5th intercostal space)). The key specifies the right fifth intercostal space, but standard surface anatomy places tricuspid auscultation at the lower left sternal border, typically the fourth or fifth space. The stem does not specify a local lecture convention. Clarify that convention or key the left parasternal site.
- **dq-g-gr1** (keyed A T, B T, C T, D F, E T). The keyed respiratory division includes the lungs wholesale even though much of the airway inside them is conducting. The key also places trachea in the upper tract and excludes the larynx from the lower tract. Specify the course classification, and distinguish the gas-exchanging zone from the whole lung.
- **dq-g-gr13** (keyed D (upper sympathetic trunk ganglia)). The trachealis has parasympathetic supply via vagal and recurrent laryngeal fibres as well as sympathetic input. The sympathetic route is appropriate for vascular tone, but the stem combines vascular supply with smooth-muscle innervation without specifying which autonomic division.
- **dq-g-grs10** (keyed B (the rima glottidis nearly doubles)). Male laryngeal growth after puberty lengthens the vocal folds and lowers voice pitch. Gray's nearly doubled measurement refers to the sagittal diameter of the thyroid cartilage, not a fixed measure of the rima glottidis, whose opening varies with position. Specify the structure and measurement.
- **dq-g-grs28** (keyed E (sympathetic fibres from T2–4)). In humans, direct sympathetic innervation of airway smooth muscle is sparse or absent, and adrenergic bronchodilation is largely mediated by circulating adrenaline acting on beta-2 receptors. Clarify whether the item is testing the traditional general autonomic scheme or human physiology.
- **dq-g-grg1** (keyed C (bronchi, bronchioles and lungs)). The key places the trachea in the upper tract. Standard anatomical descriptions typically include the trachea in the lower respiratory tract, making trachea, bronchi and lungs the better choice under that convention. Specify the course classification if the current key is intentional.
- **dq-g-grg7** (keyed D (Quadrangular membrane)). The quadrangular membrane is intrinsic, but the thyroepiglottic ligament also connects two laryngeal cartilages and is conventionally classified as intrinsic. Replace that distractor or specify an intrinsic membrane.
- **dq-g-grg18** (keyed E (left phrenic nerve)). Both phrenic nerves supply parts of the right-crural sling around the oesophagus. The left supplies right-crural fibres to the left of the oesophagus, and the right supplies fibres on its right. Specify the side of the oesophagus to make the keyed answer unique.
